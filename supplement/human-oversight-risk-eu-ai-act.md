# EU AI Act: scope, transitions and sector rules (supplement to chapter 37)

Checked: October 2026, against the consolidated text of Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 as amended to 27 July 2026, <https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2024/1689/2026-07-27/eng>.

This is a reading aid, not legal advice, and classification, whether a change is substantial and which date applies are legal questions for counsel.

## Who the Act reaches

The Act reaches providers placing systems on the Union market or putting them into service, and providers placing general-purpose models there, wherever located (Art. 2(1)(a)). It reaches Union deployers and providers and deployers outside it whose outputs are used there (Art. 2(1)(b) to (c)). It excludes areas outside Union law and preserves Member States' national security competences (Art. 2(3)).

A provider develops or commissions a system or general-purpose model and places it on the market or puts the system into service under its own name or trademark, paid or free (Art. 3(3)). A deployer uses a system under its authority, outside personal nonprofessional activity (Art. 3(4)). Reading both definitions, an organization building and running its own system can hold both roles (Arts. 3(3), 3(4)).

## High-risk by intended purpose

Intended purpose includes context and conditions in the provider's instructions, promotional or sales materials, statements and technical documentation (Art. 3(12)). Annex III includes systems for evaluating natural persons' creditworthiness or establishing their credit scores, except financial-fraud detection (Annex III, point 5(b)). Check intended purpose against Annex III.

A substantial modification follows market placement or service entry and was not foreseen or planned in the initial conformity assessment. It affects Chapter III Section 2 compliance or changes the assessed intended purpose (Art. 3(23)).

## Duties by stage

**Before use.** Establish, document and maintain high-risk system risk management, identifying foreseeable risks and targeted measures (Art. 9(1), (2)). Systems training models with data need training, validation and testing datasets meeting quality criteria (Art. 10(1)). Prepare technical documentation before market placement, enable automatic lifetime logging, and make operation transparent enough for deployers to interpret outputs (Arts. 11(1), 12(1), 13(1)). Systems must be designed so that natural persons can effectively oversee them while they are in use (Art. 14(1)). Design for appropriate accuracy, robustness and cybersecurity throughout the lifecycle (Art. 15(1)). Providers ensure Section 2 compliance, arrange conformity assessment before market placement or service entry, and establish a quality management system (Arts. 16, 17(1)).

**In use.** Deployers take technical and organizational measures for use according to instructions (Art. 26(1)). Deployers assign human oversight to people with competence, training, authority and support (Art. 26(2)). Deployers monitor operation against the instructions for use (Art. 26(5)). Providers keep the automatic logs when those logs are under their control (Art. 16(e)). Deployers keep the automatic logs under their control for a period appropriate to intended purpose, at least six months unless applicable law provides otherwise (Art. 26(6)).

Deployers tell people when Annex III high-risk systems make or assist decisions about them (Art. 26(11)). Affected people can obtain clear, meaningful explanations of the system's role and decision's main elements when decisions based on Annex III system outputs, except point 2, have legal or similarly significant effects they consider adverse to health, safety or fundamental rights (Art. 86(1)).

**At a change.** Any distributor, importer, deployer or other third party is considered a provider of a high-risk system when it puts its name or trademark on a high-risk system already placed on the market or put into service (without prejudice to contractual arrangements), makes a substantial modification to one that remains high-risk, or modifies the intended purpose of a system not previously high-risk so that it becomes high-risk (Art. 25(1)). The initial provider, in summary, then loses that role (Art. 25(2)). A substantial modification requires a new conformity assessment for an already assessed high-risk system (Arts. 3(23), 43(4)). The promotion record in chapter 37's duties table is evidence of what changed that the assessment can draw on, not the assessment itself.

**At an incident.** A serious incident is a system incident or malfunction leading directly or indirectly to death, serious health harm, serious irreversible disruption of critical infrastructure management or operation, infringement of Union obligations protecting fundamental rights, or serious property or environmental harm (Art. 3(49)). Deployers suspecting use presents a risk notify the provider or distributor and market surveillance authority and suspend use (Art. 26(5)). Deployers identifying serious incidents immediately inform the provider first, then the importer or distributor and authorities (Art. 26(5)). Providers report serious incidents involving high-risk systems on the Union market to market surveillance authorities where they occurred, not later than 15 days after the provider or, where applicable, the deployer becomes aware of the serious incident (Art. 73(1), (2)).

## Sector rules

For Article 6(1) high-risk systems related to Annex I Section B products, only Articles 6(1), 60a and 102 to 112 apply (Art. 2(2)). Articles 57 to 59 apply only insofar as high-risk requirements have been integrated into that product legislation (Art. 2(2)).

High-risk system providers under sectoral Union quality-management law may include the Act's quality-system aspects within those systems (Art. 17(3)). Financial-institution providers under Union financial services governance requirements meet that duty through those rules, except paragraph 1 points (g), (h) and (i) (Art. 17(4)).
